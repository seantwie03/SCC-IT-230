Exercise

Remove the LVM Setup Exercise

Task

Take apart the LVM setup the last three exercises built on servera, in the reverse order you built it: unmount /data and remove its /etc/fstab entry, then remove the logical volume, the volume group, and the physical volumes, and wipe both disks. When you finish, /dev/sdb and /dev/sdc are blank again and /etc/fstab holds only its original lines.

Host
servera
Prerequisite
Extend a Logical Volume Exercise

  1. Unmount /data and remove its /etc/fstab entry

    sudo -i
umount /data
vim /etc/fstab

    In vim, type /lv_data and press Enter to jump to the line you added in the Create a Logical Volume Exercise, press d twice to delete it, then type :wq and press Enter. 

    cat /etc/fstab
systemctl daemon-reload
rmdir /data

    cat shows only the original lines, ending with the /dev/sr1 entry. rmdir removes the empty mount point, which no later exercise uses.

    Remove the entry before the next reboot An /etc/fstab line for a device that no longer exists makes the next boot wait 90 seconds for it, then stop in emergency mode.

  2. Remove the logical volume

    lvremove /dev/vg_data/lv_data

    lvremove asks Do you really want to remove active logical volume vg_data/lv_data? [y/n]: Type y and press Enter; it answers Logical volume "lv_data" successfully removed. Removing a logical volume is irreversible: everything in it, including bigfile, is gone. If it reports contains a filesystem in use instead, /data is still mounted; go back to the first step. 

    lvs

    lvs now lists only the system's own root and swap.

  3. Remove the volume group

    vgremove vg_data
vgs

    With no logical volumes left in it, vgremove removes vg_data without asking, and vgs lists only rhel_servera.

  4. Remove the physical volumes on /dev/sdb1 and /dev/sdc1 

    pvremove /dev/sdb1 /dev/sdc1
pvs

    pvremove reports Labels on physical volume "/dev/sdb1" successfully wiped. and the same for /dev/sdc1, and pvs lists only /dev/sda3. The partitions themselves are still there until the next step.

  5. Wipe the /dev/sdb and /dev/sdc disks

    wipefs --all /dev/sdb
wipefs --all /dev/sdc
lsblk /dev/sdb /dev/sdc

    For each disk, wipefs lists the signatures it erased: the GPT header at the start of the disk, its backup copy at the end, and the protective MBR. lsblk then shows both disks with no partitions, as they were before the Initialize a Physical Volume Exercise.
